# v0.8.0 Weapon Modifications Design

## Summary

OWCA v0.8.0 lets a player attach supported upgrades to a particular owned weapon and see a deterministic, explainable final profile without mutating the shared equipment catalogue. It also makes weapon craftsmanship a true per-instance choice: every owned ranged or melee weapon can independently be Poor, Common, Good, or Best craftsmanship, while grenades and missiles remain quantity-based records without craftsmanship modifiers.

The milestone covers rules data, compatibility, calculations, inventory mutation, persistence and migration, interoperability, Manage Loadout presentation, Armoury presentation, printable dossiers, tests, and development documentation. Campaign advancement, requisition, acquisition economics, combat ammunition expenditure, temporary combat effects, and new catalogue breadth remain outside v0.8.0.

## Product Contract

A player can:

- own two copies of the same base weapon with different craftsmanship, notes, and installed modifications;
- select any supported ranged or melee weapon and set its individual craftsmanship to Poor, Common, Good, or Best;
- inspect compatible upgrades for a specific owned weapon;
- understand why a known upgrade is incompatible;
- install or remove a supported upgrade atomically;
- compare the immutable base profile with the calculated final profile;
- see which craftsmanship rule or installed upgrade changed each statistic or quality;
- save, load, exchange, and print the modified weapon without losing instance identity or audit history; and
- count a valid definition with no declared weight as `0 kg` while still treating a missing definition as unresolved.

The existing creation Loadout page remains intentionally concise. It may display final weapon results already present in state, but modification administration belongs to Manage Loadout.

## Supported Scope

The first v0.8.0 release supports the five existing Core upgrade definitions already present in `equipment_catalog.json`:

- `mono_melee_upgrade`;
- `compact_upgrade`;
- `red_dot_laser_sight`;
- `telescopic_sight`; and
- `tripod_bipod`.

Only verified mechanical facts, concise original summaries, and existing source references may be encoded. Compatibility or effects that cannot be established from an authorized source remain explicitly unsupported rather than inferred. This milestone does not import additional rulebook prose or artwork.

All `ranged_weapon` and `melee_weapon` definitions are eligible for per-instance craftsmanship. The `grenade_missile` category is excluded, including both thrown grenades and missile ammunition. A melee weapon with an alternate thrown use, such as a knife, remains a `melee_weapon` and is therefore eligible.

## Architecture

### Ownership and authority

`EquipmentDataRepository` remains the immutable source of base weapon and upgrade definitions. `CharacterState.owned_items` remains the authoritative record of what a character owns. An owned weapon stores its base definition reference, its per-instance craftsmanship, and the stable IDs of installed modifications; it never stores a copied final profile.

`CharacterInventoryService` remains the only mutation boundary used by presentation code. It delegates compatibility and final-profile decisions to pure weapon-modification logic, then updates state and audit history only after the requested operation validates completely.

`WeaponModificationCalculator` is a deterministic domain service. It receives an owned item, immutable catalogue definitions, and versioned craftsmanship rules. It returns compatibility results, base and final profiles, calculated weight, installed-upgrade details, situational effects, warnings, errors, and an ordered explanation. It does not mutate the state or catalogue.

`CharacterInventoryCalculator` consumes the weapon result when projecting owned inventory. Encumbrance, UI summaries, interoperability previews, and dossier exporters consume that shared projection instead of implementing their own modifier rules.

### Dependency direction

The dependency flow is:

1. versioned JSON rules and immutable catalogue definitions;
2. repository validation and lookup;
3. pure craftsmanship, compatibility, and modification calculation;
4. inventory mutation and aggregate inventory projection;
5. persistence, UI, interoperability preview, and dossier presentation.

No domain rule depends on a scene, control, filesystem path, editor plugin, or exporter.

## Catalogue and Rules Data

### Upgrade definitions

Each supported `weapon_upgrade` definition gains a structured modification contract containing:

- stable compatibility selectors such as allowed categories, weapon classes, families, required qualities, excluded qualities, excluded definition IDs, and conflicting modification IDs;
- a deterministic pipeline order;
- zero or more typed profile or weight operations;
- zero or more concise situational effects that do not rewrite a profile statistic; and
- the existing source reference.

Compatibility fields are optional only when their absence has a safe, defined meaning. An upgrade with incomplete required compatibility data is rejected by catalogue validation and cannot be installed.

Supported generic effect operations include:

- adding or multiplying a numeric statistic;
- adding or removing a weapon quality;
- modifying the numeric bonus of a parseable damage expression;
- adding an upgrade's declared weight;
- multiplying the weapon's weight; and
- adding a concise situational rule result to the explanation without changing a base statistic.

Any integer-producing multiplier declares its rounding rule in data. The calculator does not guess how range, magazine capacity, Damage, or another integer field should round.

### Craftsmanship rules

Craftsmanship effects live in versioned rules data rather than UI callbacks or upgrade-specific branches. Rules can produce profile changes, weapon-quality changes, or situational attack modifiers as supported by the verified source. Craftsmanship is applied as a distinct source in the explanation so it cannot be confused with an installed upgrade.

The calculation order is:

1. copy the immutable base definition and profile;
2. validate the owned weapon and resolve any legacy catalogue alias;
3. apply the per-instance craftsmanship rule;
4. validate the complete installed-modification set;
5. apply compatible modification effects by declared order, then stable ID as a tie-breaker;
6. apply explicit rounding and legal lower bounds; and
7. produce the final profile, final weight, situational effects, and explanation.

An omitted weight on a valid base or upgrade definition contributes `0 kg`. A missing referenced definition remains unresolved and prevents a complete calculation.

### Legacy craftsmanship variants

The catalogue currently contains seven craftsmanship-specific definitions with `base_definition_id`:

- `laspistol_common`;
- `lasgun_good`;
- `flamer_common`;
- `shotgun_common`;
- `heavy_stubber_common`;
- `missile_launcher_common`; and
- `chainsword_common`.

Their stable IDs remain valid for existing rules and saves. They become explicit legacy aliases and resolve to their base definition for modification calculations. Normal Armoury and inventory selection present the base weapon once and use instance craftsmanship, so a Good M36 lasgun and a Good las carbine are created through the same workflow.

The owned item's `craftsmanship` field is authoritative after materialization. A legacy alias may supply a default when an older starting grant is first materialized, but it does not override an existing owned instance. Existing documents are not silently rewritten merely to replace an alias ID.

New starting-grant data may declare craftsmanship separately from the base definition ID. Existing grant IDs and legacy aliases remain accepted for backward compatibility.

## Character State and Interoperability

### State version 5

Character state advances from version 4 to version 5. Every owned item gains:

```json
"modification_ids": []
```

For `ranged_weapon` and `melee_weapon` instances, the array contains unique supported `weapon_upgrade` IDs. Other categories require an empty array. The saved order records the player's installation order for audit readability; calculation order remains canonical and data-driven.

Existing versions 1 through 4 remain accepted migration inputs. Migration to v5 adds empty modification arrays without changing document IDs, instance IDs, definition IDs, craftsmanship, custody, provenance, notes, or quantities. The migration report states that weapon modification state was initialized.

Inventory event snapshots gain the v5 modification array. Existing events migrate with an empty array. A new `modification` event type records the complete post-operation item snapshot, a UTC timestamp supplied by the caller, and a concise reason. Events explain current state but are not replayed to reconstruct it.

### Public envelope

The character interoperability schema advances from `1.3.0` to `1.4.0`. The authoritative character block contains instance craftsmanship and modification IDs. Calculated previews may include base profile, final profile, final weight, installed-upgrade display records, situational effects, and calculation steps, but consumers must treat these as disposable projections.

Unknown consumer-owned `extensions` remain preserved unchanged. Save As and Duplicate retain their existing identity semantics. Atomic writing, validation, backup rotation, and recovery behavior remain unchanged.

### Validation

Loading or saving rejects:

- duplicate modification IDs on one weapon;
- missing or non-upgrade modification definitions;
- modifications on non-weapon items;
- incompatible installed combinations;
- malformed compatibility or effect data;
- invalid craftsmanship values; and
- quantity other than one for ranged or melee weapon instances.

Legacy documents remain loadable under their declared supported versions. A future unsupported version remains rejected.

## Mutation and Audit Behavior

`CharacterInventoryService` adds explicit install and remove operations. Each operation receives the state, catalogue and rules repositories, weapon instance ID, modification ID, reason, and UTC timestamp.

Installation validates the target instance, target category, upgrade definition, current set, proposed complete set, duplicate status, compatibility, and timestamp before changing state. Removal validates the target and installed ID before changing state. A no-op request returns a clear successful or invalid result as appropriate but never fabricates an audit event.

Successful installation or removal updates only the selected instance, appends one `modification` event with the complete post-operation snapshot, emits `state.changed`, and returns the fresh weapon calculation. Failure leaves both the item and event history byte-for-byte unchanged.

Craftsmanship updates continue through the existing item-update boundary. For eligible weapons, the service validates the proposed craftsmanship by calculating the complete weapon with its installed modifications before committing the change. A craftsmanship change that would make an installed set invalid is rejected atomically with an actionable explanation.

Starting-loadout rebuilds preserve modifications on intact instance IDs. If changed creation inputs replace an obsolete starting weapon, its modifications are not silently transferred to the new instance; the normal inventory history continues to explain the replacement.

## Compatibility and Error Model

The calculator returns a structured compatibility result for every candidate upgrade:

- `compatible: bool`;
- stable reason code;
- concise player-facing reason; and
- the fields or installed IDs that caused incompatibility when safe to expose.

Compatibility is evaluated against the canonical base weapon definition, current craftsmanship when relevant, and the complete proposed modification set. Known incompatible upgrades remain visible in Manage Loadout but disabled with their reason. Missing or malformed rules do not become permissive defaults.

Calculation errors distinguish:

- missing base definition;
- missing upgrade definition;
- invalid owned-item shape;
- unsupported target category;
- incompatible modification;
- conflicting modification combination;
- unsupported or malformed effect;
- unparseable statistic required by an effect; and
- invalid craftsmanship data.

The UI presents concise recovery guidance. Logs and test diagnostics may include stable IDs, but player-facing messages do not expose raw schema terminology unnecessarily.

## User Interface

### Manage Loadout

Every ranged or melee weapon card in maintenance mode shows:

- base weapon name and instance craftsmanship;
- current final profile and final carried weight;
- a compact base-versus-final comparison for changed fields;
- installed modifications with Remove actions;
- a searchable or selectable list of supported upgrades;
- compatible upgrades with an Install action;
- incompatible upgrades disabled with a concise reason; and
- an expandable calculation explanation identifying craftsmanship and each upgrade separately.

The add-equipment workflow selects a base weapon and an initial craftsmanship rather than offering craftsmanship-specific duplicate definitions. Existing owned legacy-alias instances remain editable and calculate through their canonical base definition.

Weapon modification controls stack vertically at narrow widths. Search, comparison, Install, and Remove actions remain reachable at the supported 960x650 minimum window without horizontal scrolling.

### Creation Loadout

Creation mode retains its three existing primary sections and does not expose installation, removal, compatibility administration, legacy IDs, or audit controls. If a loaded draft already contains modifications, its concise item profile and review summary use the final calculated result.

### Armoury

The read-only Armoury continues to show base weapon and upgrade definitions. Normal weapon browsing hides legacy craftsmanship aliases to avoid duplicate choices. Upgrade details show supported compatibility and concise effects without copying rulebook prose.

## Inventory, Summary, and Dossier Projection

`CharacterInventoryCalculator` includes a `weapon` result for resolved weapon instances. Carried weight uses the final calculated weight, including installed upgrade weight and multipliers. A valid base or upgrade definition with no weight contributes zero; only a missing definition makes the weight total partial.

Character summaries and interoperability previews show the final profile while retaining the base profile and calculation steps for inspection. Dossier output lists instance craftsmanship, installed upgrade names, final statistics, and an intelligible base-to-final explanation. Long modification names wrap rather than shrink below readable print size, and continuation pages remain available for large loadouts.

The dossier does not print the full inventory event ledger. It also does not imply current magazine contents, temporary bonuses, attack rolls, or combat state.

## Godot AI Plugin Boundary

The installed Godot AI plugin may be used when it materially helps inspect scene structure, exercise the real Godot editor, or validate UI behavior. The v0.8.0 runtime and domain design do not depend on the plugin. Existing local addon files and unrelated `project.godot` or export-preset changes are preserved and are not included in v0.8.0 commits unless separately authorized.

Automated test failure in an editor-control tool is not treated as product failure without corroborating application evidence. Manual smoke-test results state exactly which tool and renderer were used.

## Verification Strategy

Implementation follows test-driven development. Each behavior test is observed failing for the intended missing behavior before production code is added.

### Data and repository tests

- accept all supported structured upgrade definitions and craftsmanship rules;
- reject duplicate IDs, broken base aliases, malformed operations, missing rounding declarations, broken references, and invalid compatibility selectors;
- verify the five supported upgrade definitions and four craftsmanship tiers have source-grounded rule data; and
- parse every shipped JSON file and validate catalogue cross-references.

### Domain tests

- calculate Poor, Common, Good, and Best instances of representative ranged and melee weapons;
- keep grenades and missiles ineligible for craftsmanship modifiers;
- resolve a legacy Good M36 lasgun through the M36 base definition;
- allow a Good las carbine even though no craftsmanship-specific catalogue definition exists;
- apply each supported upgrade to compatible representative weapons;
- reject representative category, class, family, quality, definition, duplicate, and conflict failures;
- prove deterministic ordering independent of saved installation order;
- preserve immutable base profiles;
- explain every changed field and situational effect;
- use zero for omitted valid weights and unresolved status for missing definitions; and
- include upgrade weight and multipliers in encumbrance.

### State, service, and persistence tests

- observe atomic install, removal, no-op, and failed-operation behavior;
- prove independent modification and craftsmanship state for two copies of one weapon;
- record deterministic modification audit snapshots;
- migrate v4 state and schema `1.3.0` documents to v5 and `1.4.0` in memory;
- round-trip v5 saves without losing IDs, aliases, modifications, extensions, or audit history;
- reject malformed, incompatible, future-version, and non-weapon modification data; and
- preserve atomic save, backup, and recovery behavior.

### UI and export tests

- select base weapons with any eligible craftsmanship;
- hide legacy variants from normal selection while retaining exact lookup support;
- install and remove compatible upgrades from one owned weapon;
- display disabled incompatible upgrades with reasons;
- retain the simplified creation workflow;
- keep primary controls reachable at 960x650 and readable at 1280x800;
- project modified profiles through summaries and interoperability output; and
- generate a non-empty A4 dossier containing craftsmanship, installed upgrades, final statistics, and wrapped explanations.

### Completion checks

Run the focused tests first, then every repository Godot test, shipped-JSON parsing and schema validation, headless project parse/startup, `git diff --check`, the real-renderer dossier test, manual UI checks at both supported window sizes, and visual inspection of the generated PDF. Use the Godot AI plugin for editor-side inspection when it improves confidence, while retaining a normal application smoke check as the product evidence.

## Documentation and Versioning

The development version advances from `0.7.1-dev` to `0.8.0-dev`. Update:

- `project.godot` when the unrelated local edit can be preserved safely;
- `README.md`;
- `OWCA/ROADMAP.md`;
- `OWCA/CHANGELOG.md` under Unreleased;
- `ARCHITECTURE.md`;
- `OWCA/data/schema.md`;
- `JSON_INTEROPERABILITY.md`;
- formal schemas and examples; and
- contributor or release instructions only where verified behavior changes them.

Documentation distinguishes planned, implemented, automated, manually checked, and visually inspected behavior. It does not claim release, CI, manual UI, or PDF verification until that check has actually run.

## Acceptance Criteria

v0.8.0 is complete when:

- every owned ranged or melee weapon can independently use any supported craftsmanship tier;
- grenades and missiles cannot receive craftsmanship modifiers or installed weapon upgrades;
- normal selection no longer limits craftsmanship choices to seven duplicate catalogue variants;
- the seven legacy variant IDs remain compatible with existing saves and rules;
- all five supported upgrades can be installed on compatible individual weapons and removed safely;
- known incompatible combinations are filtered or blocked with explanations;
- base statistics remain immutable and the final calculation is deterministic and explainable;
- craftsmanship, modifications, and notes remain independent for two weapons with the same base definition;
- valid omitted weights count as zero and missing definitions remain unresolved;
- saves, migrations, interoperability previews, summaries, and dossiers preserve and expose the new state correctly;
- the Manage Loadout and creation flows remain usable at 960x650;
- all automated checks pass;
- the actual Godot UI has been smoke tested at 960x650 and 1280x800;
- the generated A4 dossier has been rendered and inspected; and
- project documentation accurately records the verified development state.

## Explicit Non-Goals

v0.8.0 does not add:

- XP or requisition costs for modifications;
- acquisition rolls or campaign economy;
- temporary ammunition, jams, firing modes, attack rolls, or damage rolls;
- temporary buffs or encounter state;
- homebrew upgrade import;
- additional supplement weapon or upgrade catalogues;
- campaign advancement or Comrade advancement;
- journal features; or
- a new runtime dependency on the Godot AI plugin.
